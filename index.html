I received my master's training in Medical Biochemistry and Molecular Biology at Peking University, supervised by Prof. Yongping Cui and Associate Professor Dr. Weimin Zhang. Following my master's degree, I remained in the same research group as a Research Assistant for two years, building on my master's research and further optimizing the established approaches, while also contributing to several collaborative projects, including single-cell lineage tracing and cholangiocarcinoma.

My research interests mainly include Cell and Molecular Biology, Biochemistry, Imaging, AI, Tumor, Immunology. I am particularly interested in using imaging approaches to directly visualize cells and tissues, and molecular approaches to trace the origins and mechanisms of diseases and life.

    Three-dimensional characteristics of T cells and vasculature in the development of mouse esophageal cancer
    Shanshan Bi, Yueguang Wu, Ning Ding, Yan Zhou, Huijuan Liu, Yongjia Weng, Qiqin Song, Li Zhang, Matthew Yibo Cheng, Heyang Cui, Weimin Zhang*, Yongping Cui*
    iScience, 2024
    Developed a 3D imaging pipeline to map stage-specific microenvironmental changes during mouse ESCC development, revealing early vascular remodeling, dynamic T-cell infiltration, and their spatial association, particularly at the inflammatory stage. By overcoming the spatial limitations of conventional 2D histology, this study established a three-dimensional framework for investigating the esophageal cancer microenvironment across different pathological stages.
    IRF2BPL transcriptionally regulates IGFBP2 to promote tumor progression and suppresses immune cells infiltration in esophageal squamous cell carcinoma
    Yueguang Wu†, Heyang Cui†, Longlong Wang, Ning Ding, Yongjia Weng, Yikun Cheng, Shanshan Bi, Heng Xiao, Mingwei Gao, Huijuan Liu, Qiqin Song, Weimin Zhang*, Yongping Cui*
    Oncogene, 2025
    Revealed a noncanonical role of the E3 ubiquitin ligase IRF2BPL as a transcriptional activator of IGFBP2, thereby promoting ESCC progression and reducing immune-cell infiltration within the tumor microenvironment. These findings expanded the functional understanding of ubiquitination-related proteins and identify the IRF2BPL–IGFBP2 axis as a potential therapeutic target for simultaneously limiting tumor malignancy and improving the immune contexture of ESCC.
    Evolutionary adaptation and asymmetric inheritance of polyploid giant cancer cells in esophageal squamous cell carcinoma
    Qiqin Song, Mingwei Gao, Yongjia Weng, Xuehan Zhuang, Yueguang Wu, Heyang Cui, Ning Ding, Longlong Wang, Shanshan Bi, Li Zhang, Weimin Zhang*, Yongping Cui*
    Cancer Letters, 2025
    Investigated how polyploid giant cancer cells (PGCCs) survive chemotherapy-induced stress and give rise to heterogeneous progeny in ESCC. By integrating multicolour high-resolution 3D/4D live-cell imaging with quantitative subcellular analysis, this study revealed dysregulated cell-cycle progression, noncanonical nuclear dynamics, and asymmetric organelle inheritance. These findings provided new insight into the cellular adaptations that enable PGCC persistence and progeny diversification under therapeutic stress.
    MST1 interactomes profiling across cell death in esophageal squamous cell carcinoma
    Li Zhang, Mingwei Gao, Yueguang Wu, Huijuan Liu, Xuehan Zhuang, Yan Zhou, Qiqin Song, Shanshan Bi, Weimin Zhang*, Yongping Cui*
    Medical Review, 2024
    Characterized MST1 interactomes across apoptosis, autophagy, and pyroptosis in ESCC using APEX2-based proximity labeling combined with immunoprecipitation–mass spectrometry (IP–MS), revealing distinct interaction networks potentially associated with mitotic regulation, DNA repair, chromatin remodeling, and cell-death resistance.
